## What the mark is

A **heart-shield crest**. An inverted heart forms a shield or coat-of-arms silhouette,
drawn as a thick pink outline with a small teardrop loop at the top apex. Inside it sits
the wordmark **SHUMI**, set in a heavy high-contrast serif — very thick stems against
hairline serifs, in the Didone family — filled black with a thin pink outline and a soft
black drop shadow. A broad pink **swoosh** sweeps across the lower third of the wordmark,
also carrying a black drop shadow.

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| Container | Heart-shield crest, pink outline, ~14px stroke at 1300px wide |
| Apex | Small teardrop / flame loop at the top point |
| Wordmark | SHUMI, high-contrast Didone-style serif, all caps, tight |
| Wordmark treatment | Black fill, thin pink outline, soft black drop shadow |
| Swoosh | Broad pink sweep across the lower third, black drop shadow |
| Ground | White |

**The logo pink is not usable for text or buttons.** A pink this bright fails WCAG AA
against white by a wide margin. Every concept therefore carries two pinks: the logo pink
for the mark and for large decorative moments, and a deepened sibling that carries
meaning. Both are recomputed once the real file lands.

## Rules carried into every concept

1. **The outline-and-drop-shadow treatment belongs to the logo alone.** It never appears
   on buttons, cards, headings or photography. It is the mark's signature; repeating it
   cheapens it.
2. **The wordmark serif is not the website's typeface.** It is dramatic and high-contrast;
   the client asked for soft and approachable. The site's display face is chosen as a
   deliberate counterpoint so the logo stays the boldest piece of type on any page.
3. **The swoosh is the one element worth extending** into the design language — it is the
   only part of the mark that is distinctly SHUMI's and is not a generic crest device.
4. **The crest reads small badly.** At favicon and mobile-header sizes the heart-shield
   plus outlined wordmark plus swoosh is too much detail. A simplified mark — the crest
   alone, or a monogram — should be produced alongside the full lockup.

## Note for the design team

A crest with an outline, an inline and a drop shadow is a **2000s-era logo construction**.
It will fight any contemporary design language placed around it. The three concepts all
assume the logo is used **as supplied and unchanged**, and are built to sit beside it
rather than argue with it.

If SHUMI ever wants the site to read as a serious global platform, a light modernisation
of the mark — same heart-shield, same swoosh, same pink, but the outline and drop shadow
retired and the wordmark redrawn — would do more for the brand than anything on the
website. That is a separate conversation and not assumed by any concept here.
