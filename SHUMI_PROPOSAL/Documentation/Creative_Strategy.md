# SHUMI — Creative strategy

TedcanLabs · September 2026

---

## 00. The one thing that must be settled first

🔴 **Is SHUMI a global organisation, or a Cape Verdean-American organisation in
Brockton, Massachusetts?**

Earlier material in this project described SHUMI as serving the Cape Verdean diaspora in
a specific American city. This brief describes a United States-based organisation serving
women worldwide. **Those are different organisations and they need different websites** —
the difference is structural, not cosmetic.

Everything below assumes the global reading, because that is what this brief asks for.
If the local reading is the true one, the specificity is a *strength* and this strategy
should be narrowed rather than built. **This question is worth five minutes of SHUMI's
time before anyone designs anything.**

---

## 01. What SHUMI actually has to overcome

A young organisation with global ambitions has one structural problem: **it cannot yet
prove scale.** No attendance figures, no press, no partner logos, no impact numbers that
have been audited.

The conventional answer is to borrow the visual grammar of scale anyway — a statistics
band, a press marquee, a logo wall. Our primary reference does exactly this, and it has
earned the right to. SHUMI has not.

**So the strategy is to buy credibility with craft instead of with numbers.**

A site that is composed, beautifully typeset, photographically serious and structurally
intelligent reads as a serious organisation without asserting one untrue thing. That is
the whole bet, and it is why the recommended direction is editorial-led.

---

## 02. The creative idea

# THE WORLD THROUGH HER.

Not "women around the world" — which is a category. **"The world through her"** — which
is a lens.

The site moves constantly along one axis:

```
HER  →  HER STORY  →  HER COMMUNITY  →  HER OPPORTUNITY  →  HER IMPACT  →  THE WORLD
```

Every piece of content on the site sits somewhere on that line, and the design's job is
to make the line visible. A story is not a blog post; it is the near end of a chain that
reaches the far end. A statistic is not a tile; it is the far end of a chain that starts
with one person.

**Why this is the right idea for SHUMI specifically:** it solves the credibility problem
structurally. An organisation that cannot yet say "we reached 40,000 women" *can* say
"here is one woman, and here is how far her work travels." The idea scales up as SHUMI's
evidence does — the same architecture that works at three stories works at three hundred.

---

## 03. What the site must do in eight seconds

A visitor arrives from Instagram, on a phone, knowing nothing.

1. **Who is SHUMI?** — answered by photography and one editorial statement, before any
   reading is required.
2. **What does SHUMI do?** — answered by named, concrete programmes, not abstractions.
3. **How do I take part?** — answered by one primary action, repeated down the page.

**The site fails if** it reads as a local charity, if the pink becomes a wall, if a
65-year-old has to pinch to read it, or if it claims a global footprint SHUMI cannot
back up.

## 04. Reference analysis

### moderndaywife.com — the primary reference

Analysed from its actual markup, not from impression.

**Structure:** four nav items (Who We Are · Events with four named children · Magazine ·
Contact). Homepage runs: all-caps hero → who we are → a four-figure statistics band →
mission → five pillar blocks → a scrolling adjective animation → testimonials → a
press-logo marquee → footer.

**What is genuinely worth taking:**

| Principle | Why |
|---|---|
| **Named events are navigation destinations** | Individual events have their own identity and URL rather than being rows on a listing page. This converts far better, and it is the single best idea on the site. |
| **Event-first architecture** | The whole site is organised around "when can I come." Correct priority for an organisation that gathers people. |
| **Multiple content ecosystems** | Events, magazine, podcast, social — each reinforces the others rather than competing. |
| **Attributed testimonials** | Real names against real quotes. The credibility mechanism is right. |

**What must not be carried over:**

| Pattern | Why not |
|---|---|
| The statistics band (8000+, 55K, 250K+, 10+) | SHUMI has no such figures and inventing them is out of the question |
| The press-logo marquee | SHUMI has no press yet; a row of logos it has not earned is a lie with a design system around it |
| The all-caps hero | Harder to read, and dated |
| **Four typefaces** (Montserrat, Darker Grotesque, Lato, Open Sans) | That is not a type system, it is an accident |
| **Heading order running h1 → h3 → h4 → h2** | A screen-reader user navigating by heading gets a scrambled outline. A real accessibility defect, and a reminder that a site can look premium and be badly built |
| The scrolling adjective animation | Motion budget spent on decoration that says nothing |

**The strategic lesson:** Modern Day Wife converts because *the event is the product and
the site knows it.* Take that clarity. Leave the credibility scaffolding.

### The wider reference landscape

Studied as categories rather than copied: global women's organisations, international
NGOs, premium social-impact bodies, contemporary editorial publications, cultural
institutions, documentary storytelling sites.

**The pattern worth stealing from cultural institutions and editorial publications, and
almost absent from the NGO category:** they trust a single image and a single sentence to
carry a whole screen. NGO sites tend to crowd — three columns of cards, four statistics,
a carousel — because they are anxious about proving relevance. That anxiety is legible,
and it reads as smallness.

**SHUMI should be the confident one in its category.** That is available, cheap, and
almost nobody is doing it.

## 05. Design philosophy

1. **Human before corporate.** People and stories before organisational language.
2. **Editorial before template.** Composition, hierarchy and scale contrast — not
   card-grid repetition.
3. **Global without being generic.** Global is carried by *authentic specificity* —
   real women in real environments — never by landmarks, flags or a spinning globe.
4. **Impact without NGO cliché.** No handshakes, no volunteer group shots, no
   beneficiary framing. **Women as agents, never as recipients** — nobody in any frame
   is being helped.
5. **Beautiful but purposeful.** Every visual decision serves understanding, emotion,
   navigation, trust or conversion.
6. **Confident, not loud.** Authority comes from typography, scale, composition and
   restraint.

## 06. The three directions, and the recommendation

Three genuinely different concepts were developed and compared in full (see
`concepts/README.md` for the scored comparison).

| | **01 SHUMI EDITORIAL** | **02 SHUMI GLOBAL** | **03 SHUMI HUMAN** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Position | The world's editorial voice for women | A global network connecting women and opportunity | A deeply human platform of real stories |
| Feels | Cultural, intelligent, sophisticated | Connected, dynamic, discoverable | Warm, personal, intimate |
| Hero | Editorial statement over immersive imagery | World → woman → community → impact | One woman, one story, then the world |
| Strength | Highest credibility; degrades honestly with no data | Highest ceiling; scales to a platform | Highest conversion and warmth |
| Weakness | Coldest; depends on photographic quality | **Looks worst when empty** | Least differentiated; weakest at "global" |

### Recommended: **EDITORIAL × GLOBAL IMPACT**

Roughly **70% premium editorial · 20% global impact platform · 10% intimate human
storytelling** — a balance, not a formula.

**Why this and not one of the three:**

- **Editorial leads** because it is the only stance that lets SHUMI look serious *today*,
  with no statistics, no press and no partner logos. It degrades honestly.
- **Global impact supplies the structure** — country as metadata on every story and
  programme, a presence section, region filters. This makes "global" a fact of the
  architecture rather than a claim in the copy, which is the hardest requirement in the
  brief.
- **Human storytelling supplies the emotional centre** — one woman told properly,
  repeatedly, is more persuasive than any grid.

**What we deliberately do not take from Concept 02:** its content debt. Animated
counters, an interactive map and a filtered programme grid all look worse empty than
absent. Those components are *designed and specified* here, and switched off until SHUMI
has something true to put in them.

## 07. What this strategy refuses to do

Recorded before build so it is agreed rather than argued later:

- **No invented statistics, countries, programmes, partners, testimonials, awards,
  funding or team members.** Where a fact is missing the design carries a visible
  placeholder — `XX+`, `[PROGRAM NAME]`, `[WOMAN'S NAME]` — and the placeholder is
  designed to look deliberate rather than broken.
- **No stat band or logo wall** until there is something true to fill it.
- **No quotation** that a named woman did not actually say, with her permission.
- **No claim of an Eventbrite integration** — the architecture is designed to accept one.

**Where SHUMI has no numbers, the honest version is also the more sophisticated one.**
An editorial statement carries a page better than four empty tiles ever could.
