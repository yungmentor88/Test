# SHUMI — Cinematic motion assets

Higgsfield prompt system for the six motion assets used as visual punctuation
through the SHUMI proposal deck. TedcanLabs.

**Model:** Kling v3.0 (`kling3_0`) · `std` mode · sound off · 16:9 · 8 seconds
**Why this configuration:** `pro` mode requires a plus plan; the account is on
starter, so every asset is rendered in `std`. At 8s/16:9 this costs ~10 credits per
clip against a 205-credit balance, which leaves real headroom to regenerate anything
that comes back wrong — and with generated humans, some always do.

**Placement in the deck** — motion is punctuation, not wallpaper. Six moments only:

| # | Asset | Slide |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Global Women | 01 Cover |
| 02 | Human Connection | 07 Three ways to experience SHUMI |
| 03 | Homepage Hero | 15 Hero experience |
| 04 | Global Impact | 17 Impact experience |
| 05 | Stories | 20 Stories experience |
| 06 | Closing | 32 Closing |

## The house style clause

Every prompt carries the same closing direction so the six clips read as one film:

> Warm natural available light, shallow depth of field, rich film-like colour grade,
> fine grain, anamorphic cinematic feel. Emotionally understated and dignified.

And the same negative direction, which is the more important half:

> No text, no logos, no captions, no watermarks, no glowing graphics, no maps, no
> corporate stock posing, no exaggerated smiling at camera, no plastic skin, no
> distorted hands or faces.

**Why the negatives matter more than the positives.** Three failure modes destroy
this kind of footage: AI faces that read as synthetic, stock-photo body language, and
NGO cliché (the glowing globe, the connecting light lines, the white saviour framing).
Each is named explicitly in every prompt rather than hoped away.

## VIDEO 01 — GLOBAL WOMEN
*Cover. The first thing SHUMI's leadership sees.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Subject** | Four women in four different parts of the world, one brief shot each — a West African woman crossing a market street; a South Asian woman turning toward light in a doorway; an East Asian woman working at a bench, hands busy; a Latin American woman on a rooftop above a city. |
| **Environment** | Real working environments — market, doorway, workshop, rooftop. No studios, no landmarks, no tourist geography. |
| **Camera** | Documentary. Eye level. |
| **Lens** | Wide-to-normal, anamorphic character. |
| **Camera movement** | Gentle handheld drift with a slow push in on each subject. |
| **Lighting** | Golden hour and natural available light throughout. |
| **Mood** | Dignified, understated, four separate lives that rhyme. |
| **Composition** | Each subject placed off-centre; room in frame. |
| **Visual style** | Documentary montage, rich film grade, fine grain. |
| **Duration** | 8s |
| **Aspect ratio** | 16:9 |
| **Negative** | House negatives, plus: no tourist landmarks, no flags, no travel-brochure geography. |

**Direction note.** The montage structure is the point — it says *many places* without
a single map. Four lives, cut together, doing the job a globe graphic would do badly.

## VIDEO 03 — HOMEPAGE HERO
*Slide 15. Typography sits over this — the composition must leave room.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Subject** | One woman alone at the edge of a bright open space, small in frame, three-quarters back to camera, turning slowly to look into the distance. |
| **Environment** | A large open space — soft empty wall meeting sky. |
| **Camera** | Cinematic wide. |
| **Lens** | Wide, anamorphic. |
| **Camera movement** | Very slow steady dolly push forward. |
| **Lighting** | Warm early morning light, soft haze. |
| **Mood** | Quietly powerful, hopeful, unresolved. |
| **Composition** | **The left two thirds of frame is deliberately empty.** The headline lives there. |
| **Visual style** | Muted sophisticated grade, shallow depth, fine grain. |
| **Duration** | 8s |
| **Aspect ratio** | 16:9 |
| **Negative** | House negatives, plus: no crowds, no lens flares, no graphics overlay. |

**Direction note.** Negative space is a *hard requirement*, not a preference. A headline
over a busy frame is the single most common way a cinematic hero fails, and it fails on
contrast, which is measurable. The composition is briefed so the type never needs a scrim
to be legible.

## VIDEO 04 — GLOBAL IMPACT
*Slide 17. Replaces the glowing-globe cliché with a human one.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Subject** | One continuous move outward in scale: close on the hands and face of one woman teaching → pulls back to the room of women listening → continues back through a doorway → the building within its wider neighbourhood at dusk. |
| **Environment** | Interior teaching space opening into an exterior community at blue hour. |
| **Camera** | Single unbroken take. |
| **Lens** | Normal to wide. |
| **Camera movement** | Continuous slow backward dolly and crane, one move, no cuts. |
| **Lighting** | Warm practical interior giving way to blue-hour exterior. |
| **Mood** | One person's work rippling outward. |
| **Composition** | Scale expands through the shot — the frame answers "how far does this reach?" |
| **Visual style** | Rich cinematic grade, shallow to deep focus. |
| **Duration** | 8s |
| **Aspect ratio** | 16:9 |
| **Negative** | House negatives, plus: **no glowing globe, no map graphics, no connecting light lines, no network diagram, no data visualisation.** |

**Direction note.** This is the asset most at risk of NGO cliché. The brief solves
"global impact" with a camera move rather than a graphic: the pull-back *is* the idea.
The negatives here are the longest in the set for exactly that reason.

## VIDEO 05 — STORIES
*Slide 20. The emotional centre of the deck.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Subject** | An intimate portrait sequence — women looking directly into the lens, holding the gaze, small natural movements: a breath, a blink, the beginning of a smile that does not complete. |
| **Environment** | Each woman in her own real environment, softly out of focus behind her. |
| **Camera** | Locked-off portrait, subject centred and close. |
| **Lens** | Portrait, wide aperture. |
| **Camera movement** | Almost none — a barely perceptible drift. |
| **Lighting** | Soft directional natural light, one source, real shadow. |
| **Mood** | Direct, composed, self-possessed. Not vulnerable, not performing. |
| **Composition** | Tight, eyes on the upper third, gaze straight down the barrel. |
| **Visual style** | Editorial portraiture, rich grade, fine grain, real skin texture. |
| **Duration** | 8s |
| **Aspect ratio** | 16:9 |
| **Negative** | House negatives, plus: no styled fashion posing, no glamour retouching, no tearful expressions, no pity framing. |

**Direction note.** Direct eye contact is the whole asset. It is also where synthetic
faces are most exposed, so real skin texture is called for explicitly and this clip gets
reviewed hardest before it goes in the deck.

*Slide 32. Breathing room for the final statement.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Subject** | Women walking forward together through a bright open space, moving toward and past camera, unhurried and purposeful. Faces various, ages various. |
| **Environment** | Wide, bright, architectural — a concourse or open hall. |
| **Camera** | Low-ish wide, subjects approaching. |

| **Lighting** | Bright natural daylight, high key, soft. |
| **Mood** | Possibility and forward motion. Momentum, not triumph. |
| **Composition** | Upper third kept clear for the closing statement. |
| **Visual style** | Clean cinematic grade, fine grain. |
| **Duration** | 8s |
| **Aspect ratio** | 16:9 |
| **Negative** | House negatives, plus: no slow-motion hair flips, no raised fists, no marching, no protest imagery, no triumphal poses. |

**Direction note.** "Forward motion" collapses into protest or triumph imagery very
easily, and both would misrepresent SHUMI. The negatives rule out the four poses this
shot naturally drifts toward.

## Honest limits on these assets

1. **These are concept films, not documentary evidence.** They are AI-generated and
   show no real SHUMI programme, participant or location. Anywhere they appear in the
   deck they are labelled as design exploration. They must never be presented as
   footage of SHUMI's work.
2. **`std` not `pro`.** The account plan caps quality at `std`. For production these
   would be re-rendered at higher quality, or — better — replaced with real footage.
3. **Real footage beats all of this.** The correct end state is a filmed day with real
   women. These assets exist to sell the *idea* of the motion system, and the deck says so.
